## 2. Google "Twiddlers": what they are, and what they are not

On November 4, Search Engine Roundtable reported that Olivier de Segonzac of Resoneo published a **list of about 120 Twiddlers**, which he grouped into categories. The story builds on Google internal documents that surfaced in earlier leaks and in the U.S. Department of Justice case.

**What a Twiddler is (as described in the leaked-document analyses):**
- After Google's main ranking step scores a list of results, **Twiddlers are rules or systems that adjust that list**. They can **boost, demote or filter** results using signals such as freshness, engagement or manual and algorithmic filters, and Search Engine Roundtable mentions NavBoost as an example.
- Resoneo's earlier explainer describes them as part of a framework called **Superroot**, working on a **ranked sequence of results** rather than on each page alone.
- Categories in the new list include names such as **Expertise, Authenticity, Topic authority, Authority data, Web health, Hero document, Domain classification, RankBrain** and some internal-sounding names like "Starbow". I only saw part of the list.

**Important warnings:**
- **Google has not confirmed this list.** It comes from third-party analysis of leaked material.
- The leaked documents are from **earlier years**. Systems may have changed since.
- A name in a list does **not** tell you how much it matters, how it is triggered, or whether it is still active.
- SEO commentators who have analysed Twiddlers (for example Marie Haynes) describe them as **interpretation**, and mention that some assumptions, such as how snippets or meta descriptions are used, are guesses.

**What this means for you (my analysis):**
- The names are a **useful reminder** that ranking is not one score. After initial ranking, **freshness, diversity, spam filtering and quality adjustments** can still change where a page lands.
- It supports the usual advice: **real expertise, original information, clear authorship, a healthy site and a recognisable brand**.
- **Do not try to "optimise for a Twiddler".** You cannot see or measure them.

**What to do:**
- [ ] Use the list as a **reading prompt**, not a checklist.
- [ ] Keep the basics strong: unique content, author details, fast and crawlable pages, accurate dates, and no spam tactics.
- [ ] Avoid paying anyone who claims to "hack" a specific Twiddler.
