## TL;DR

The most useful new data this week comes from **Adobe**: shoppers who click through from AI assistants to U.S. retail sites generated **83% more revenue per visit** than other visitors in September 2026. That is a strong sign that **AI traffic is small but valuable**, especially going into the holiday season. It also comes with important caveats, which I explain below.

---

## 1. Adobe: AI-referred shoppers are worth 83% more per visit

From Adobe Digital Insights (report published **October 29, 2026**, covering **September 2026**), based on analysis of more than **1 trillion visits** to U.S. retail sites, 100 million products and 18 retail categories:

- AI traffic (people clicking a link in an AI assistant) grew **71% year over year**, and **1,772% since October 2024**, when Adobe started tracking it.
- AI-referred visits generated **83% higher revenue per visit** than non-AI traffic, the **largest gap of 2026** so far.
- They **converted 90% better**. A year earlier, in **August 2025**, AI visits converted **9% worse** than non-AI visits.
- Other engagement differences reported in coverage of the report: **73% more time on site**, **25% more pages per visit** and **53% higher add-to-cart rate**.
- Earlier in the year, Adobe's August 19 report showed a smaller gap: **53% more revenue per visit** and **60% better conversion** for AI traffic (based on July data).
- Adobe expects the revenue-per-visit advantage to widen further during the holiday season (a **forecast**, not a result).

### Read this carefully before changing any budget

- **U.S. retail only.** It says nothing directly about services, B2B, local businesses or other countries.
- The comparison group is **"non-AI traffic"**, a blend of sources such as email, paid search and social. It is **not** a comparison against Google organic search alone.
- It measures **quality per visit, not volume**. AI referral traffic is still much smaller than classic search.
- Adobe sells analytics software, so it has an interest in the topic. Independent commentators also note that some engagement figures are **not dated** in Adobe's report.
- Referral tracking has been messy (see my October 19 post on ChatGPT's missing tags), so some AI visits may be **hiding in Direct**.

**What this means (my analysis):** If a similar pattern holds for your site, a smaller number of AI visitors may be worth more than their numbers suggest, because they often arrive **after comparing options in the chat**.

**What to do:**
- [ ] In GA4, create a segment or channel group for **AI referrers** (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot and others) and compare **conversion rate and revenue per session** to your other channels.
- [ ] Don't compare AI traffic only with Google organic. Compare it with **each channel separately**.
- [ ] Check **Direct traffic** growth on pages that AI tools tend to cite.
- [ ] Add "How did you hear about us?" to forms and checkout.
- [ ] Report **quality metrics** (conversions, revenue, leads) for AI visits, not just sessions.

## 2. "Citation readability": most retail homepages are only partly visible to AI

Adobe also reports that the average U.S. retail homepage scored **63%** on its **citation readability** measure in September, which Adobe interprets as **more than a third of homepage content not being visible to the AI systems and agents** that send valuable shoppers to retailers. One secondary write-up lists category scores of about **79% for electronics, 55% for grocery and 40% for general merchandise** (I could not verify those category numbers against Adobe's own page).

**Caveat:** This is **Adobe's own metric**. I do not know the exact method, so treat the numbers as a **rough signal**, not an industry standard.

**What to do (my recommendations, not Adobe's):**
- [ ] Make sure key product and service information is in **plain HTML**, not only inside images, scripts, tabs or pop-ups.
- [ ] Write clear **product and service descriptions**: what it is, who it is for, price or price range, shipping or delivery, returns, and contact details.
- [ ] Use accurate **structured data** (Product, Organization, LocalBusiness, FAQ where appropriate).
- [ ] Keep **Merchant Center feeds** consistent with what is on your site.
- [ ] Add a short **FAQ** on important pages that answers the questions customers actually ask.

## 3. Reddit is testing ads in its search results (November 3)

According to coverage that cites Search Engine Roundtable and Social Media Today, Reddit said on November 3 that it is testing **ads inside its own search results** with a **limited group of advertisers**, matched to the search. Reddit describes its search pages as high in purchase intent. The same coverage notes Reddit's overall search traffic is **falling as Google's AI features answer more questions on the results page**. (Earlier this month Search Engine Roundtable reported Reddit's stock dropping after its CEO confirmed declining Google Search traffic.)

**What this means (my analysis):** Reddit is one of the most-cited sources in AI answers, yet it says it gets **less traffic from Google**. For brands, being **mentioned** on Reddit can influence AI answers, but that does not guarantee clicks.

**What to do:**
- [ ] Check how your brand is discussed on Reddit and other forums, and correct factual errors politely and transparently.
- [ ] Don't spam communities. Genuine, helpful participation works better than promotion.
- [ ] Remember the reported **Reddit RSS shutdown on November 13**, if you use it for monitoring.

## 4. Google Ads and Merchant Center expand AI content disclosure labels (November 5)

Search Engine Roundtable reported that Google expanded its **AI content label** to more regions and updated two help documents: **"Use AI content label settings and disclosures"** in Google Ads and the **product data specification** in Merchant Center. I only saw a short summary, not the full documents.

**What to do (ecommerce and advertisers):**
- [ ] If you use AI-generated images or text in ads or product feeds, **read Google's updated documents** and check whether labels apply in the regions you target.
- [ ] Keep your own record of which assets are AI-generated.

## Key takeaway

AI search may send **fewer visitors, but more valuable ones**. The sensible response is not to abandon SEO, but to **measure AI traffic separately**, make your key information easy for machines to read, and keep building a brand that is mentioned and trusted across the web.

## This week's action checklist

- [ ] Segment AI referrals in GA4 and compare revenue per session across all channels
- [ ] Review key pages for information hidden in images, scripts or tabs
- [ ] Align Merchant Center feeds with on-site product details
- [ ] Check Merchant Center diagnostics after Google's Nov 6 issue
- [ ] Review Business Profile messaging and response time
- [ ] Check Reddit and forum mentions of your brand
- [ ] Verify any Search Console anomalies against Analytics before reporting
- [ ] Re-check Google's Search Status Dashboard
