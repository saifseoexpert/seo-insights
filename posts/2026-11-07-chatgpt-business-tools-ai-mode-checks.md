## TL;DR

The most interesting story of the week: **OpenAI appears to be building a Search Console-style tool for ChatGPT**, where verified businesses can see how often they are mentioned and clicked. That could give us **first-party AI visibility data**, something Google's AI reports still do not offer for clicks. Also: Google Shopping dominates the new European product box, and Google AI Mode is now asking some users to "continue with this search", possibly to block bots.

## 1. OpenAI is building analytics for businesses in ChatGPT (spotted November 6)

Per Search Engine Roundtable, which credits a LinkedIn post by Juozas Kaziukenas:

- A banner invites businesses to **"Claim your business in ChatGPT"** and lists three functions: see **how often your business is mentioned in ChatGPT conversations**, **add your latest products and keep your business info up to date**, and let a **business agent answer customer questions** with context you control.
- The analytics part reportedly measures **impressions and clicks**: how many times a business appeared in ChatGPT responses and how many clicks went to its website. It tracks them **daily and shows a chart**.
- It is **part of a larger "Business Hub"** feature and appears available **only to businesses that have verified ownership**, for example by connecting a Shopify store.
- It reportedly **does not show prompts, user data, competitor benchmarks or rankings**. So it won't tell you *why* mentions rise or fall, but combined with outside tools that track prompts it could give accurate first-party data on whether your **GEO/AEO work is paying off**.
- The feature is described as a **work in progress**. The author saw first steps as early as August, and it appears **close to launch**.
- It appears **separate from ChatGPT ads** and aimed at **organic** responses.
- PPC Land, covering the same finding, notes a naming risk: "ChatGPT Business" is already the name of OpenAI's workplace subscription, a different product.

**Caveats:** This comes from **one person's screenshot and description**. OpenAI has not announced it in the sources I read, so the name, launch date and features could change.

**What to do:**
- [ ] **Don't build reports around it yet.** It has not launched.
- [ ] Make sure your **business details are consistent**: name, address, products, prices, website and descriptions.
- [ ] If you sell online, check that your store platform (such as Shopify) is **connected to the accounts you control**, since verification reportedly depends on proving ownership.
- [ ] **Start a baseline now**: note how often your brand appears in ChatGPT answers for your main topics, so you can compare when official data arrives.
- [ ] Keep your **GA4 channel groupings** updated for AI referrals, and add a "How did you hear about us?" field to forms.

## 2. Google Shopping dominates the new European product box (BrightEdge, reported November 6)

Background: Google dropped its **free product listings in the EEA** because of the Digital Markets Act (I covered this on October 22). Google had described this as the **biggest reduction in search quality in its history**.

BrightEdge data, as reported by Search Engine Roundtable (markets analysed: **Spain, Italy, France and Germany**):

- **Google Shopping appears 16 to 35 times as often** as the next-largest comparison service in the new box.
- **71%** of comparison site appearances land in the **top four positions**: from **61% in France to 76% in Spain and Italy**.

A European retail analyst quoted in the article, Mike Ryan, said the change was meant to bolster comparison-shopping-service visibility, but that he has never seen a single service **other than Google Shopping** get the spotlight. That is **his observation**, not a verdict.

**What to do (for clients selling in Europe):**
- [ ] Keep Merchant Center feeds accurate (titles, prices, stock, shipping).
- [ ] Check traffic and revenue from EEA countries separately in Search Console and analytics.
- [ ] Don't mistake a drop in free listings for a ranking penalty or spam update effect.
- [ ] Review whether paid Shopping ads or comparison services make sense for your margins.

## 3. Google AI Mode asks some users to "Continue with this search?" (November 6)

Damien Andell spotted a new prompt in AI Mode that reads "Continue with this search?", with a note that **malicious queries could result in unsafe or harmful actions**, before running a search.

Barry Schwartz's view: it may be a way for Google to **block bots and scrapers** from AI Mode. He says that is his **gut feeling**, and notes Google has recently used sign-in or "continue" buttons to verify humans. He also joked it could encourage more searches. **Google has not explained it** in the sources I read.

**Why it matters (my analysis):**
- Many AI visibility tools collect data by **automatically querying AI search**. If Google adds more checks, some tools' data may become **less complete or less reliable**.
- This fits the pattern of **tracker data problems** I covered on October 21 and 28.

**What to do:**
- [ ] Ask your AI visibility vendors how they collect data from AI Mode and what happens when Google blocks them.
- [ ] Treat tracker numbers as **estimates**, and cross-check with Search Console and analytics.

## Key takeaway

Measurement is catching up with AI search. OpenAI looks set to give businesses their own mention and click data, while Google is tightening bot controls around AI Mode. The practical move is to **build clean baselines now** so that when official data arrives, you can tell what actually changed.
