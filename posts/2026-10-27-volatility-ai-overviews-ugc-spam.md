## TL;DR

Three things moved this week. (1) Rankings shook again on **October 24**, with no confirmed update. (2) New data says **AI Overviews now appear on 80%+ of Google searches**. (3) Google updated its guidance for platforms and tools that let users publish pages, with a new rule of thumb: **only index content meant for Search.**

## 1. Ranking volatility gets heated on October 24 (unconfirmed)

Search Engine Roundtable's Barry Schwartz reported on October 25 that many third-party tracking tools showed **significant volatility on Saturday, October 24**, alongside heavy chatter from SEOs about ranking and traffic changes. His read: Google likely released some **ranking adjustment on Friday into Saturday**. **Google has not confirmed anything.**

Context from the same article:
- It follows unconfirmed movement on **October 16** and **October 20-21**.
- The September 2026 spam update ran from **September 24 (about 12:00 pm ET) to October 8 (4:37 am ET)**. SER counted three volatility waves: **Sept 25-27, Sept 30 and Oct 4-6**.
- Rank trackers have struggled for about a month, but SER says many report having **mitigated** the issues by now.

What site owners reported on forums (anecdotal, not Google statements):
- Discover traffic spiking then fading to very little, with some saying it lasts **under 6 hours** per article.
- Google News showing old stories.
- Traffic drops "across the board" for some, and rankings "bouncing around" for others.
- One hotel owner described **low website traffic but good walk-in business**, a gap between web numbers and real-world takings. It is one person's observation, but it is a useful reminder to track business results, not just analytics.

**What to do:**
- [ ] Annotate **Oct 16, Oct 20-21 and Oct 24** in Search Console and analytics.
- [ ] Wait several days before changing pages. Chasing a moving target is how sites make things worse.
- [ ] Compare **Search, Discover and News** separately in Search Console.
- [ ] Cross-check any rank tracker drop against Search Console impressions and clicks.
- [ ] Track leads, calls and sales next to traffic.

*I did not re-check Google's Search Status Dashboard today. Check it for any confirmed entry with a start time.*

## 2. AI Overviews now appear on more than 80% of searches

In a report on October 26, Search Engine Roundtable summarised recent data from Semrush, SE Ranking and seoClarity:

- AI Overviews now show for **over 80%** of Google searches, up from about **50% in June 2025** and about **15%** the year before that.
- **SE Ranking:** AI Overviews on **82.5% of U.S. brand searches**.
- **seoClarity:** over **62%** of queries, ranging from **55% to 84% depending on industry** (a figure from about a month ago).
- Semrush published a breakdown by industry and query type.

**Why numbers differ:** On October 26 I wrote about Comscore data showing AI Overviews in 39.4% of U.S. **desktop** searches in June 2026. These figures use different samples and methods (observed searches vs tracked keyword sets, desktop only vs all devices, different dates), so **do not compare them directly**. The direction is the same: AI answers are now the norm.

**What to do:**
- [ ] Check what AI Overviews say about **your brand** and which sources they cite.
- [ ] Build content that is **worth citing**: original data, clear answers, real expertise and named authors.
- [ ] Measure impressions, branded searches and leads, not just clicks.

## 3. Google updates guidance for platforms that let users publish pages

On October 26, Barry Schwartz reported that Google updated its help page **"Prevent user-generated spam on your site and platform."**

What changed (as reported):
- The page now specifically covers **"tools that let users publish shared pages, generated content, or web apps."**
- A new section called **"Only index content meant for Search"** tells platforms to separate content meant for **public discovery** from content meant only for **direct access** by people with a link.
- For pages made for sharing, collaboration or utility rather than search discovery, Google recommends applying **`noindex`** (robots meta tag or `X-Robots-Tag` header) or **blocking crawling in robots.txt by default**.
- Platforms should **verify their site or subdomain in Search Console** to receive manual action and security alerts, regularly check the **Page Indexing** and **Search performance** reports for unexpected spikes, and set alerts for sudden growth in public pages or for a few users generating a large share of indexable URLs.
- Google's stated reason: to help platforms keep user-created pages that are not meant for Search out of the index and **detect spam earlier**.

Community reaction (opinions, not Google statements): Marie Haynes asked whether this means new manual actions are coming for AI tools and app-building platforms, and Lily Ray speculated that Google may be dealing with a new kind of spam produced when AI and social tools make it easy to create indexable user content at scale.

**Who this affects:** Anyone running a site or platform with user accounts, shareable links, generated outputs or published mini-apps. It may also matter if you use **AI or site-builder tools that create public share links**, because you should know whether those pages are indexable and whether you want them to be.

**What to do:**
- [ ] List every feature on your site that creates a **public URL** (shared pages, generated results, user profiles, mini-apps).
- [ ] Decide for each one: should it appear in Google? If not, add `noindex` (a robots.txt block alone does not remove a page that is already indexed).
- [ ] Verify the site in Search Console and watch **Page Indexing** for unexplained spikes.
- [ ] Keep any page you *do* want indexed original, useful and moderated.

## 4. Discover and image notes (headline-level)

I could confirm the headlines and short summaries for these, not the full details:

- **Discover sources button:** Google is reportedly **testing disabling clicks** on the sources button in Discover article cards, which normally opens a screen listing the source articles and links (SER, October 26). If confirmed, it would reduce another route to publishers.
- **JPEG XL:** Search Engine Roundtable reported on October 23 that **Google Images and Discover do not support the JPEG XL format**. Use widely supported image formats for pages you want to appear there, and check Google's image documentation before converting.
- **Business Profile:** Google is testing new **advanced performance reports** for Business Profiles (October 23).
- **AI Overviews:** citation cards with **AI-generated summary snippets** were reported on October 23.

## Key takeaway

Google is moving in two directions at once: **more AI answers on the results page**, and **stricter expectations about what is allowed into the index**. The safest strategy is the same as before: publish fewer, better pages, control what you do not want indexed, and measure real business results.
