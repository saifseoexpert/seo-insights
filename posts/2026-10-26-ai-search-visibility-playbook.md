## TL;DR

There was no confirmed Google update over the weekend, so today I'm pulling together the **numbers** behind AI search from the last six weeks and turning them into a practical playbook. The short version: AI answers are in a big share of searches, **question-style queries trigger them most**, and measurement tools are catching up.

---

## 1. How big is AI search now?

- **Comscore** (reported by Search Engine Land): AI Overviews appeared in **39.4% of U.S. desktop searches in June 2026**, up from **25.8%** a year earlier. Bing's Copilot Search appeared in **17.3%**. U.S. desktop search volume was still **up 8%** compared with 2024. YouTube and Reddit were among the most cited sources, and some sites help power answers **without receiving a visible citation**.
- **Two studies in Search Engine Journal** (summarised by Ignite Visibility): **68%** of U.S. Google searches are now zero-click, versus **49%** in 2019. More than **1 in 6** AI Mode searches include voice, image or video, and follow-up questions in AI Mode are up **40%**.

**Caveat:** Studies measure different things. "Zero-click" definitions vary, and I have not checked the methodology of each study.

## 2. What triggers an AI Overview?

The same Search Engine Journal studies, as summarised in the roundup, found:

| Query pattern | AI Overview trigger rate |
|---|---|
| Starts with "why" | **92.3%** |
| Starts with "what" | **85.7%** |
| Starts with "who" | **68.4%** |
| Four-word searches | **48.1%** |

Also, **75.2%** of AI Overviews were weighted toward **evergreen** content.

**What this suggests:** Optimising only for short, fragmented keywords is less effective. Pages that **answer a complete question directly** and stay accurate over time are the ones AI systems can use.

## 3. AI Mode and user satisfaction (one study)

A study reported by Search Engine Land found that, for people using Google AI Mode:

- **0.92 fewer** search sessions per day
- Clicks to external sites down **18.8%** (news sites **-12.5%**, Reddit **-21.2%**, Wikipedia **-9.9%**)
- **34%** gave negative feedback, **29%** gave positive feedback
- Top complaints: less control, difficulty reaching websites directly, and limited links or source variety
- Use of Bing, DuckDuckGo or Yahoo rose **11.2%** among people told they had to use AI Mode

This is one study, so treat it as a signal, not a verdict. It is, however, consistent with Google's October 15 move to add **more and higher inline links** in AI Mode and AI Overviews (covered in my October 19 post).

## 4. Better measurement tools are arriving

- **Search Console multimodal filter** (rolling out globally from **September 24**): under the Web search type, it separates visits from **Google Lens, Circle to Search, image uploads and Chrome's "Search this image"**. It applies to both the Search results and Generative AI reports. If your site relies on product photos, infographics or other visuals, it is your first look at "search by image" traffic.
- **Bing Webmaster Tools Topics Portfolio** (beta, October 13): inside the AI Performance report, it groups the queries where your site is cited in AI answers into themed clusters, with demand, competitiveness and number of cited sites. A **Top Opportunity Queries** list shows high-volume queries where competitors are cited and you are not. In effect, it is a free content-gap list.
- **Google Analytics hostname allowlist**: lets you filter out traffic from hostnames you have not approved, and you can also block traffic with a missing hostname, which usually signals spam. This helps keep reports clean while you investigate AI referral changes.

## 5. Google Search profiles: lower eligibility

On **September 16**, Google lowered the follower threshold for claiming a **Search profile** to **10,000** (across YouTube, Instagram, X or TikTok) for **U.S.** brands, publishers and creators. The update also adds multi-account support and a cleaner profile layout. Claim at **creators.google/profile**.

**Note:** The announcement refers to U.S. eligibility, so businesses elsewhere may not qualify yet. Check Google's page for current availability.

## The playbook

**1. Write for real questions.**
- Turn key topics into question headings (why / what / who / how).
- Put the **direct answer in the first one or two sentences**, then add depth, examples and sources.

**2. Keep evergreen pages current.**
- Update facts, prices and recommendations, and link older pages to newer ones. Updating the date alone is not enough.

**3. Be present where AI systems already look.**
- Reddit, YouTube, Facebook, Quora, Instagram, LinkedIn and Medium were the most-cited platforms in the SE Ranking study I covered on October 24. Publish useful answers there, not just links.

**4. Measure more than clicks.**
- Track impressions, branded searches, AI report data, direct traffic and leads, since AI answers can influence people without sending a click.

**5. Use free gap data.**
- Check the Bing Topics Portfolio and Search Console filters for topics where you should be cited but are not.

**6. Keep your data clean.**
- Add a GA4 hostname allowlist and annotate known tracking changes (such as the ChatGPT referral issue from October 8-15).

## This week's action checklist

- [ ] Pick your top 5 pages and rewrite their headings as questions with a direct answer first
- [ ] Update one evergreen page with fresh facts and examples
- [ ] Open Search Console, check the multimodal filter and the AI report
- [ ] Check Bing Webmaster Tools > AI Performance > Topics Portfolio (beta)
- [ ] Set up a GA4 hostname allowlist
- [ ] Check whether your brand or clients qualify for a Google Search profile
- [ ] Review which social platforms are cited for your brand in AI answers
