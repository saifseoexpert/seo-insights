## 2. Confirmed vs unconfirmed: how to read volatility

This month Search Engine Roundtable reported unconfirmed volatility on **October 16, October 20-21 and October 24**. None of these has appeared as a confirmed entry in the sources I checked. The **September spam update** was the last confirmed ranking update in those sources.

Here is a simple method (my own framework, not an official one):

1. **Check the dashboard first.** A real core or spam update has a start time and a status. No entry means "unconfirmed".
2. **Check your own data, not just trackers.** Rank tracker data has been unreliable since mid-September (see my October 21, 28 and 30 posts). Look at Search Console impressions, clicks and average position for the same pages and dates.
3. **Split by surface.** Compare **Search, Discover and News** separately. A Discover-only drop is not a web ranking update.
4. **Split by page type.** Which pages moved: blog posts, product pages, templated pages, pages with AI-written text? Patterns tell you more than totals.
5. **Check what you changed.** Look at your own releases, migrations, plugin updates and server changes around the same dates.
6. **Wait 7-14 days** before making big changes, and annotate dates in your analytics.
7. **Keep a record.** Note the date, what moved, and what you did, so you can compare after any confirmed update.
