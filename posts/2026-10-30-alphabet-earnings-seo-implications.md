## 1. Alphabet's Q3 2026 results (reported October 28)

From Alphabet's official earnings release (SEC filing):

- Alphabet revenue rose **25% to $127.6 billion**.
- **Google Search & other** grew **12%** (about **$63.35 billion**), and the company said the **year-over-year Search query growth rate is the highest in the last five years**.
- CEO Sundar Pichai said AI investments are driving Search query growth, **including commercial queries**.

**The growth rate is slowing.** Using the same line from Alphabet's earlier releases:

| Quarter | Google Search & other revenue growth |
|---|---|
| Q1 2026 | **19%** |
| Q2 2026 | **17%** |
| Q3 2026 | **12%** |

From the earnings call highlights (Yahoo Finance summary):
- **Retail** was the largest contributor to Search growth, followed by **finance**.
- Pichai said AI Mode queries are on average **three times longer** than traditional searches, and **one in five** global AI Mode searches use **voice, images or documents**.
- Alphabet's CFO said most AI Mode queries are **incremental**, and could create monetization opportunities over time, but that **lower monetization in AI Mode affected Search growth** this quarter.

Financial press coverage (for example CNBC) reported that revenue and earnings per share missed Wall Street estimates and that Search revenue came in below the analyst forecast. Figures differ between outlets because some use adjusted earnings. I am reporting this for context, not as investment commentary.

### What this means for SEO (my analysis, not Alphabet's statements)

1. **Search is not shrinking.** More queries are being asked. But Alphabet's numbers say nothing about **how many clicks go to websites**, so don't read this as good news for organic traffic.
2. **Write for longer, conversational queries.** If AI Mode questions are three times longer, pages that answer multi-part questions ("best X for Y situation, and what should I check first?") are better matched than pages built around one short keyword.
3. **Images, voice and documents matter.** With one in five AI Mode searches using non-text input, descriptive alt text, clean product images and clear page structure help. The Search Console multimodal filter (covered October 26) is how you start measuring this.
4. **Expect more experiments with ads in AI answers.** Because AI Mode is monetized less today, expect Google to keep testing ways to earn from it. Search Engine Roundtable's monthly report mentioned a test of text link ads in AI Mode (headline only; I have not read the detail).
5. **Retail and finance are growing, so competition there is rising.** For ecommerce, keep product feeds, reviews and structured data accurate. For finance and other high-stakes topics, strong expertise, named authors and clear sourcing matter. Search Engine Land published a piece on E-E-A-T on October 28 (headline only).

## 2. Update: Semrush confirms "poisoned" Google results

On **October 27**, Search Engine Land reported that Semrush confirmed Google is returning inaccurate results to some automated ranking tools. This updates my October 28 post, where I described Semrush's earlier statement as saying the effect was limited.

What the report says:
- Semrush described two types of poisoned pages: **irrelevant results** (sometimes on page one) and **partially inaccurate results**, where a page looks normal at first but contains loosely related results further down.
- Example: a search in Brazil for a specific baby stroller returned a dental supply store's equipment page, because both used the same Portuguese word.
- **Facebook, Instagram, YouTube and Quora** appeared more often in suspected poisoned results than in other results for the same keywords.
- BrightLocal said the problem mainly affected rankings **below position 10**, but Semrush, AccuRanker and Nozzle each documented inaccurate results on **page one** too.

I could only read the search-result excerpt of this article, not the full text.

**What to do:**
- [ ] Keep verifying ranking drops in **Search Console** before reporting them.
- [ ] Keep the data-quality note in client reports for **Sept 13 to mid-October** and extend it if you see odd results afterwards.
- [ ] Treat sudden jumps from unexpected sites (especially social platforms) in tracker data with suspicion.
