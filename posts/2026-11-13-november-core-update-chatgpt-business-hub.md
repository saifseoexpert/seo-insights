## 1. Google November 2026 core update: the confirmed facts

From Search Engine Roundtable, Search Engine Journal and Google's own Search Status Dashboard entry:

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | November 2026 core update |
| Started | **Tuesday, November 10, 2026, about 10:06 am Pacific (about 1:07 pm Eastern)** |
| Expected length | **Up to about 2 weeks** (so likely to finish around Thanksgiving, with some sources saying about November 24) |
| Scope | **Global, all languages** |
| Google's description | "A regular update designed to better surface relevant, satisfying content for searchers from all types of sites" |
| Blog post or new guidance | **None published**, according to Search Engine Journal. No specific ranking system, content type or query category was named |
| Status | **Rolling out, not complete** (as of November 13) |

Context:
- It is the **third core update of 2026** and the **eighth ranking update** on the dashboard this year, a count that includes four spam updates (March, June, August, September).
- Previous 2026 core updates: **March 27 to April 8** and **May 21 to June 2**. About **six months** had passed since the last one.
- Across 23 earlier core updates, Search Engine Roundtable puts the **median rollout at 14 days**. The longest ever was **45 days** (March 2024).
- Search Engine Roundtable noted **big movements on November 8-9**, before Google confirmed anything. Barry Schwartz also suggested the unconfirmed volatility of recent weeks may have been refreshes or early signs, but that is **speculation**.
- Search Engine Roundtable describes a core update as **not a penalty**: it re-evaluates "core systems" and can promote pages as well as demote them. It can also affect **Discover** and features such as featured snippets.
- Glenn Gabe reportedly said all signs point to a **powerful update** (second-hand report of his comment).
- Google may do **periodic refreshes** of this algorithm and may not announce them (Barry's comment).

*A note on my earlier posts:* On October 31 I mentioned a prediction model that saw a likely update window from late October to mid-November. The update did arrive in that window, but one correct timing does not prove the model works.

## 2. Why the timing matters

The update may still be running during **Black Friday (November 27) and Cyber Monday (November 30)**. Ecommerce, travel and seasonal sites could see traffic swings in their busiest weeks. SiteGround's guidance also reminds site owners to rule out **technical causes** (indexing, uptime, Core Web Vitals) before blaming the update.

## 3. What to do: a calm plan

### While the update is rolling out (now until it is marked complete)
- [ ] **Do not make large changes in a panic.** Changing many things at once makes it impossible to tell what helped or hurt.
- [ ] **Record what you see**: date, pages, queries and the size of any change in Search Console, with annotations in analytics.
- [ ] **Rule out non-update causes first**: recent site changes, plugin or theme updates, accidental `noindex` tags, server errors, tracking changes and seasonal demand.
- [ ] **Check Search Console Performance with caution.** Search Engine Roundtable's video recap says Search Console had a **logging error for performance reports between November 9 and 11**, and an earlier report said the Performance report looked wrong for many sites on November 9. Don't treat those days as clean baseline data.
- [ ] **Compare Search, Discover and News separately**, because Discover is affected by core updates too.
- [ ] Keep **ads and promotions** running as planned, and don't judge organic performance on a single day.

### After Google marks the update complete
- [ ] Follow Google's own guidance, as reported by Search Engine Journal: **wait at least a full week after completion**, then compare Search Console data from that week with the week **before the rollout started**.
- [ ] Look for **patterns**, not totals: which page types, topics and queries moved.
- [ ] If you lost visibility, review those pages honestly: Is the information original and accurate? Is it written by someone with real experience? Does it answer the visitor's question better than competing pages? (These are general quality questions based on Google's long-standing advice, not new guidance for this update.)
- [ ] Improve pages **meaningfully** (better information, clearer structure, real examples, updated facts). Quick tweaks usually do not help.
- [ ] Remember that **recovery can take time**, and that Google may need a later update or refresh to reflect improvements.

### For holiday sellers
- [ ] Avoid risky changes before and during the peak: **no migrations, URL changes or template overhauls** if you can help it.
- [ ] Keep product pages, stock status, shipping and returns information accurate, and check Merchant Center diagnostics.
- [ ] Make sure your site is fast and stable under holiday traffic.

## 4. ChatGPT Business Hub is live: click data for businesses (November 10)

On November 10, OpenAI announced **"Businesses in ChatGPT"** in beta, with a **Business Hub**. Per Search Engine Roundtable's coverage of OpenAI's announcement:

- OpenAI says **1.2 billion people use ChatGPT each week**, and the hub helps businesses connect with them.
- Businesses can **claim their business**, **manage their information** and see insights on how people engage with them.
- **Analytics** include the **number of mentions and website clicks**, plus **profile views**, and "**discovery topics**", which are common themes in conversations where your website is cited.
- A tooltip on the click metric says **clicks do not confirm website visits or conversions**.
- **Verification** is by **text message or phone call to your main business number, or by DNS verification**. Barry Schwartz verified via DNS and had no data yet when he wrote, while others reported already seeing numbers.
- **Business Agents**, which let customers chat with a business to explore offerings, start with **eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in the U.S.**
- Barry said he is **nervous about people hijacking businesses** and hopes OpenAI will handle that.

**What to do:**
- [ ] Check whether the Business Hub is available for your country and business type, since the beta is limited and the Business Agent feature is U.S.-first.
- [ ] If available, **claim your business** using an account and DNS or phone details you control.
- [ ] Make sure your **business information** (name, description, services, products, prices, contact details) is accurate and consistent with your website.
- [ ] Start a **baseline**: record mentions and clicks weekly, and compare them with GA4, since ChatGPT says clicks don't confirm visits.
- [ ] Review the **discovery topics** for content ideas and gaps.
- [ ] Be careful with who has access to your DNS and business phone number.
