## TL;DR

This week's earlier posts covered rank tracker data and update rumors. ## 1. Gemini 4 Argon arrives in AI Mode

- Google announced **Gemini 4 Argon** on September 30.
- On **October 13** it became available in AI Mode for **Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers worldwide**.
- Google says it handles nuanced, multi-step requests and follow-up conversations better than before.
- Early reports say links do appear in its responses. The earlier Gemini 3.8 Flash release in AI Mode first shipped without links and citations until Google fixed it.

**What to do:** Citations and inline links are where your pages can earn visibility. Answer questions clearly, with specific and credible information, so your pages are easy to use as a source.

## 2. Google tests an AI Mode sidebar on the normal results page

On **October 14**, some users reported a left sidebar from AI Mode appearing on the standard Google results page, with chat history and a shortcut to start an AI Mode chat. Search Engine Roundtable could not reproduce it, so it appears to be a **limited test**.

**What to do:** Nothing to change yet. Just check how your key queries look in both the normal results and AI Mode.

## 3. Google's "AI contribution" pilot pays publishers very little

Search Console includes an **invite-only pilot** that pays some publishers when their content contributes significantly to AI Overviews, AI Mode and Gemini answers. According to a report by *The Information*:

- About **100 publishers** are taking part.
- Several small and mid-size sites receive **less than 0.1% of their advertising revenue** from it.
- Some larger publishers have stayed out.
- Participants see a monthly earnings figure but no breakdown of how it was calculated.

Most sites cannot join. The takeaway is that Google is beginning to put a price on how its AI uses web content, and the early price is low.

**What to do:** Don't build a business plan around AI payouts. Keep investing in direct audiences: email, social, brand searches and returning visitors.

## 4. Free product listings disappear across Europe (Digital Markets Act)

On **September 17-18**, free product listings and the "Popular products" carousels largely disappeared from Google Search across the **European Economic Area (EEA)**.

- Google's Ads Liaison **Ginny Marvin** confirmed the removal is tied to the **Digital Markets Act**.
- Productrise tracking recorded drops of roughly **90-100%** in Germany, France, Belgium, Sweden and the Netherlands, with the space going to comparison shopping services.
- **Paid Shopping ads remain.**

**What to do (for clients selling to Europe):**
- [ ] Check Merchant Center and Search Console for EEA traffic changes since mid-September.
- [ ] Don't mistake this for a ranking problem or a spam update penalty.
- [ ] Keep product feeds clean and Product schema accurate. They still support visibility elsewhere.

Related: Google updated its Merchant Center documentation to say that **poor image quality will not stop products from showing** in Search or Ads. Image quality still matters for clicks and conversions.

## 5. Local SEO: Business Profile and Maps changes

Local businesses saw several changes this month:

- Business Profile owners now have **four days to reject user-suggested edits** to their listings.
- Google Maps now **requires signing in** to read all reviews.
- Google emailed profile owners about a **spike in spam reviews**.
- Google is **testing automatic business hours**.

**What to do:**
- [ ] Check Business Profile notifications weekly. Missing the four-day window means unwanted edits can go live.
- [ ] Keep hours accurate, especially around holidays.
- [ ] Keep asking happy customers for genuine reviews, and report spam reviews.

## 6. New Search Console filters

Search Console added a **multimodal search type filter** to the Performance report in late September. This month Google also added filters that let you **group data from multiple countries into one report**, which helps businesses selling across several markets.

**What to do:** Use country grouping to compare markets, such as the EEA against the rest of the world (useful for item 4).

## Key takeaway

Search is changing in three directions at once: **more AI answers, less free visibility in some places (Europe shopping), and more rules for local profiles.** The businesses that hold up are the ones with strong brands, accurate listings and direct audiences, not just rankings.

## Action checklist for this week

- [ ] Check EEA traffic in Merchant Center and Search Console (if you sell in Europe)
- [ ] Review Business Profile notifications and edit suggestions
- [ ] Make sure hours and contact details are correct
- [ ] Review top pages for clear, citable answers (AI Mode links)
- [ ] Use multi-country grouping in Search Console
- [ ] Grow email and direct audiences alongside search
